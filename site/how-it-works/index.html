How Meetle works

The 20-second version

Sign in. Choose a username. Tap Start Chat and you're talking to one stranger. Tap Next for someone new, Stop to leave, or Add friend to keep them — quietly. If they add you back, you match. That's it. Here's each step.

Step 1

Pick a name, not a persona

Meetle needs a real account. Continue with Google, Discord or GitHub — whichever you already have. We use it to know you're a real, repeat person; we don't post anything, and we never show it to anyone. Then choose a username: 3 to 20 characters, letters, numbers and underscores. That username is all a stranger will ever see of you, so pick one you'd say out loud. There's no bio to write, no photo to choose, no profile to browse.

Step 2

Tick the box, tap Start

Meetle is 18+, so confirm your age — the Start Chat button stays off until you do. Then tap it. You'll see "Looking for someone…" while we pair you with whoever's been waiting longest. No filters, no preferences, no algorithm deciding who deserves you. (That's what "rhythms without algorithms" means.) Change your mind? Stop is right there.

Step 3

Say something

You're paired. Their username and a throwaway id like user_a83f91 sit at the top of your screen; yours sit at the top of theirs. Your account is never shown to anyone. Then it's just words — up to 1,000 characters a message, delivered the instant you hit Send. No profile to hide behind, no camera to fix your hair for.

Three buttons live under the chat the whole time

  • Next — ends this chat and finds you someone new. They see "riley_m disconnected. Click Next to find someone else." and nothing more.

  • Stop — ends the chat and takes you back to the start screen. Come back whenever.

  • Report — ends the chat immediately, records the report, and puts you back in line for someone new. You never have to explain.

Nothing you say to a stranger is saved. When the chat ends it's gone — from your screen, from theirs, and from our server, because it was never written anywhere.

Step 4 · Vibe

Tap Add friend. Tell no one.

Like them? Tap Add friend, during the chat or right after. Here's the part that makes Meetle Meetle: the other person is told nothing. Their screen doesn't change. Their button still says Add friend, exactly as if you'd never tapped yours. There's nothing to accept, no "so-and-so wants to be friends", no list of people waiting on you. Adding someone is a private decision, and it stays private unless it's mutual.

Step 5 · Meetle Magic

If it's mutual, it's magic

If they tap Add friend too — during the chat or after it — you both see the same line at the same moment: "You and QuietStorm matched as friends!" It doesn't matter who tapped first. Nobody asked, nobody got turned down, and two people who were already talking get to keep going.

After the match

Your people, your vibe

Every friend you've matched with shows up under Direct Messages — a rail on the left, visible from every screen. Those chats are saved and delivered live, so you can pick up tomorrow or next month. The top bar switches between Chat (meet someone new) and Friends. Outgrow someone? Remove friend takes them out of your Direct Messages and you out of theirs. No message is sent, no drama.

The tagline, explained

What "talk first, match later" means

"Talk first, match later" means that on Meetle the conversation comes before the decision: you talk to a stranger with no profile in the way, and you only become friends if you both choose it afterwards — by each tapping Add friend.

Most apps do it backwards. You judge a photo and three lines of bio, you match, and then you try to manufacture a conversation. Meetle flips it: the conversation is the whole first step, and the match is just what you call it when both people want it to continue. Friends, companions, or more — that part is yours to decide.

Just so we're clear

What Meetle isn't

  • Not a video chat. There's no camera and no microphone. Words only.
  • Not a dating app. No profiles, no photos, no swiping. Friends, companions or more — that's yours to decide.
  • Not anonymous in the Omegle sense. You sign in, so there's an account behind every username. Strangers still see only your username. Pseudonymous, not anonymous.
  • Not a place chats live forever. Stranger chats are never saved. Only DMs between matched friends are.
  • Not a "who liked you" list. If it isn't mutual, it isn't anything.
  • Not for anyone under 18.

On the roadmap

What's next

We're a small team building in the open, and we'd rather ship than promise, so no dates. Roughly in order: bans for accounts that keep getting reported, automatic moderation inside live chats, interest-based rooms so you can find people who want to talk about the same thing, and a couple of small games to break the ice.
