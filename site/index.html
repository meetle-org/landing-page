How it works ↓

What is Meetle?

Random text chat, one stranger at a time

Meetle is a random one-on-one text chat with strangers: you're paired with one person and talk first, and if you both tap Add friend you match and keep talking in DMs — if only one of you does, nothing happens and nobody finds out. No video, no profiles, no saved stranger chats. 18+.

Think of it as the good part of Omegle — a real person, right now, no queue of profiles to skim — with the sketchy parts taken out. Everyone signs in. Nobody has a camera on. Nobody has a bio to judge. And when a conversation is worth keeping, there's a way to keep it.

Talk first

One stranger, one text chat, in your browser. No camera, no bio. Just say something.

Match later

Tap Add friend whenever you feel it. They're never told — unless they tap it too. Then you're matched.

Nothing left behind

Real sign-in, username only, 18+. Stranger chats are never saved. Report ends a chat instantly.

How it works

Go deep or keep it chill

One stranger, one chat, and you decide how it goes. Simple, no pressure.

  1. Sign in, pick a username

    Continue with Google, Discord or GitHub and choose a username. That's your whole profile: strangers see it and nothing else.

  2. Tap Start Chat

    Confirm you're 18+, tap Start Chat, and you're paired with one stranger. Say hi. Text only, up to 1,000 characters a message. Not feeling it? Tap Next for someone new, or Stop to take a break.

  3. Add friend — quietly

    Tap it if you'd like to keep talking. They're told nothing. If they tap it too, you both see "You and QuietStorm matched as friends!" and the chat moves to your DMs.

Meetle Magic

Add friend is a secret until it's mutual

Here's the whole idea. On Meetle you can't like someone before you've talked to them — there's no one to like. You meet a stranger, you talk, and somewhere in there you decide.

Tap Add friend (we call it a Vibe). The other person isn't told. Their screen doesn't change. Their button still says Add friend, exactly the way yours did. If they tap it too, you both see it in the same moment — "You and QuietStorm matched as friends!" — and you're in each other's DMs for as long as you both want.

If they don't tap it? Nothing happens. No "seen", no rejection, no list of people waiting on a yes from you. You just keep going.

Only mutual adds become friends. Nobody ever finds out about a one-sided one.

After the match

Keep the chat rolling

If you both vibe, why stop? You've matched — now the chat has a home. Every friend gets their own thread in your Direct Messages, and those messages are saved, so you can shoot a message, share a meme, or pick up where you left off next week. Friends, companions, or something more: you decide. No algorithm nudging you along. And if it fizzles, Remove friend is one tap away. No hard feelings.

No catfish allowed

Real people. Every account is signed in.

Everyone signs in with a real account — Google, Discord or GitHub — and picks a username. That's all a stranger ever sees: your username and a throwaway id like user_a83f91 that changes every chat. No photos to judge, no bios to fake, nothing to catfish with. You're 18 or older, or you're not here. And if someone's off, tap Report — the chat ends on the spot and you're on to the next person. We don't check IDs and we don't pretend to; we do keep it honest.

Peace out

Don't feel like continuing? Tap Next and you're on to someone new in a second. Tap Stop and you're out. No explanation, no awkward goodbye, no hard feelings — the chat is gone the moment it ends, on both sides.

Meetle Magic

Want to stay connected? Tap Add friend. They won't hear a thing — unless they tap it too. Meet each other halfway and it's Meetle Magic: you're friends, and the chat gets a home in DMs. If they don't, they'll never know you tried.

Why text

No camera. Ever.

Meetle is text only — no video, no voice. Nobody's judging your lighting, your room, or your face at 11pm. You can talk from the bus, the library, or bed with the lights off. You get a second to think before you say something. And the person on the other end is reacting to what you actually said — which turns out to be a much better way to find out if you vibe.

It's how most real friendships started anyway: a group chat, a reply, a dumb joke that landed. Meetle just skips the part where you had to already know each other.

Is it like Omegle?

Like Omegle, but more

Same spark: hit a button, meet a stranger, talk to strangers online without a queue of profiles. Different rules: real sign-in, 18+, text instead of a webcam, and a way to actually keep the good ones. Omegle shut down in 2023. Meetle keeps the spark and fixes the rest.
