The 20-second version

How Meetle works

Sign in and pick a username. Tap Start talking and you're talking to one person. Leave whenever you like. Voice and video happen only if you both choose them, and video starts covered. Tap Keep in touch to keep them — quietly. If they keep you too, you can talk again any time. That's it. Here's each step.

Step 1

Pick a name, not a persona

Meetle needs a real account. Continue with Google, Discord or GitHub — whichever you already have. We use it to know you're a real, repeat person; we don't post anything, and we never show it to anyone. Then choose a username: 3 to 20 characters, letters, numbers and underscores, and not your real name. It's what the people you talk to see, so pick one you'd say out loud. There's no bio to write, no photo to choose, no profile to browse.

Step 2

Tick the box, tap Start talking

Meetle is 18+, so tick "I'm 18 or over" — Start talking stays off until you do. Then tap it. You'll see "Finding someone who wants to talk" while we pair you with whoever's been waiting longest. No filters, no preferences, no algorithm deciding who deserves you. Change your mind? Stop looking is right there.

Step 3

Say something

You're paired with one person. Their username sits at the top of your screen, with how long you've been talking — in minutes, never seconds. Yours sits at the top of theirs. Your account is never shown to anyone. Then it's just words: up to 1,000 characters a message, delivered the instant you hit Send. The first line says it plainly: nothing here is saved.

Two ways out, always in the same corner

  • Leave One tap and it's over — no confirmation, no reason. They see "riley_m headed off." and nothing more. Talk to someone new is one more tap.
  • Report Asks what happened, with one-tap answers, and "Just get me out" first for when choosing is too much. Either way the conversation ends at once, and on their side it looks exactly like you left.

Nothing you say to a stranger is saved. When the conversation ends it's gone — from your screen, from theirs, and from our server, because it was never written anywhere.

Step 4 · Games

Stuck? Play something

If the first thirty seconds are awkward — they usually are — tap Play something. Deal a question card: you both see it, either of you can skip it or deal the next, and nobody is told who did. Or pick a small game: Four in a Row, In Sync, One Word Story or tic-tac-toe. Nothing starts until the other person taps Play. Games sit beside the chat, never over it, so the conversation keeps going, and either of you can put one away any time.

Step 5 · Voice and video

Voice and video, only if you both say yes

Every conversation starts in text, and it can stay there. Anything more is something the two of you choose together.

  1. Text

    Where every conversation starts. They see your username and what you type. Nothing else.

  2. Voice

    Only when you both choose it. Until then, nobody hears a thing.

  3. Video

    Only when you both choose it, and it starts covered: all either of you sees is a blur. It uncovers when you both tap, and either of you can cover it again, instantly.

  4. Keep

    Keep in touch, if you both tap it. The only step that lasts after the conversation ends.

Nothing from a conversation with a stranger is saved, and voice and video are never recorded.

Step 6 · Keep in touch

Tap Keep in touch. Tell no one.

Like them? Tap Keep in touch, any time during the conversation. Here's the part that makes Meetle Meetle: the other person is told nothing. Their screen doesn't change. Their chip still says Keep in touch, exactly as if you'd never tapped yours. There's nothing to accept, no "so-and-so wants to keep in touch", no list of people waiting on you. Keeping someone is a private decision, and it stays private unless it's mutual.

Step 7 · Both of you

If it's mutual, it's magic

If they tap Keep in touch too, you both see the same line at the same moment: "You and maya_reads kept each other — you can talk again any time." It doesn't matter who tapped first. Nobody asked, nobody got turned down, and two people who were already talking get to keep going.

After

The people you kept

Everyone you kept each other with is on one list, The people you kept, one tap from the start screen. What you say to each other there is saved, so you can pick up tomorrow or next month. There's still no profile — just a username and when you last talked. Outgrow someone? Remove from Kept takes you off each other's lists. Nobody gets a message about it.

The tagline, explained

What "just talk" means

"Just talk" means that on Meetle the conversation comes before everything else. You talk to a stranger with no profile in the way, and anything more — voice, video, keeping in touch — happens only if you both choose it.

Most apps do it backwards. You judge a photo and three lines of bio, you match, and then you try to manufacture a conversation. Meetle flips it: the conversation is the whole first step, and keeping in touch is just what you call it when both people want it to continue. Friends, companions, or more — that part is yours to decide.

Just so we're clear

What Meetle isn't

  • Not camera-first. Every conversation starts in text. Voice and video only happen when you both choose them, and video starts covered.
  • Not a dating app. No profiles, no photos, no swiping. Friends, companions or more — that's yours to decide.
  • Not anonymous in the Omegle sense. You sign in, so there's an account behind every username. Strangers still see only your username. Pseudonymous, not anonymous.
  • Not a place chats live forever. Stranger conversations are never saved. Only messages sent after you've kept each other are.
  • Not a "who liked you" list. If it isn't mutual, it isn't anything.
  • Not for anyone under 18.

On the roadmap

What's next
